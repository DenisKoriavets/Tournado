Hi! We are developing Tournado, a web platform for organizing amateur and student esports tournaments.

Act as a skeptical Senior Business Analyst and Systems Analyst. Analyze the existing requirements and identify the core entities, lifecycle transitions, strict business rules, implicit non-functional requirements, gaps, and assumptions needed for the MVP.

## Context

Tournado supports three game disciplines:

- Counter-Strike 2 (5v5 teams).
- Brawl Stars (3v3 teams).
- Chess.com (individual 1v1).

The main user journey covers five stages: tournament creation, registration, check-in and bracket generation, match management and dispute resolution, and final results.

The project requirements are documented in `docs/`, including context, personas, user journeys, user stories, acceptance criteria, and Gherkin scenarios.

## Task

Read all files in `docs/` recursively and write the analysis in English to `docs/analysis/requirements-digest.md`. Create the output directory if necessary and leave the original files unchanged.

This is requirements analysis only. Do not design the system, propose architecture, or choose technologies.

## 1\. Entities and Their Lifecycles

Identify the key entities supported by the requirements.

For each entity, document:

- Explicitly mentioned attributes.
- States and lifecycle transitions.
- Who initiates each transition and under what conditions.
- Relevant restrictions and dependencies.
- References to the applicable user stories and scenarios.

Distinguish explicit requirements from inferred states and flag anything unspecified.

## 2\. Business Rules and Invariants

Extract strict business rules and prohibitions from the acceptance criteria and Gherkin scenarios.

For each rule, include:

- A unique ID (BR-01, BR-02, etc.).
- A precise, testable statement.
- The relevant story ID (US-XX) and scenario name or number.
- A short quotation from the requirements where possible.

Pay particular attention to registration limits, roster validation, check-in, bracket generation, match results, no-shows, score confirmation, disputes, AI-assisted decisions, permissions, and tournament completion.

Identify contradictions explicitly. Do not invent rules or treat optional behavior as mandatory.

## 3\. Non-Functional Requirements

Identify explicit and reasonably implied requirements concerning:

- Security, authentication, authorization, and public access.
- Concurrent actions and conflicting updates.
- Data integrity and result consistency.
- Deadlines and time-sensitive actions.
- External integrations and failure handling.
- AI reliability and human oversight.
- Privacy, auditability, and performance.

Label each finding as **Explicit**, **Implied**, or **Proposed MVP Assumption**. Explain its relevance and cite supporting requirements where possible.

Do not invent numerical performance targets or compliance requirements.

## 4\. Gaps and Open Questions

Identify missing, ambiguous, or conflicting requirements that could block implementation or compromise the MVP.

For each gap, include:

- A unique ID (GAP-01, GAP-02, etc.).
- What is missing or unclear.
- Relevant story and scenario references.
- Why it matters.
- The cheapest reasonable resolution for the MVP.
- Risk rating: low, medium, or high.
- Priority: before implementation, before the affected feature, or deferrable.

Focus on consequential questions such as lifecycle transitions, permissions, insufficient check-ins, bye handling, conflicting scores, missing external results, roster changes, dispute decisions, and corrections to completed matches.

Verify that each suspected gap is genuinely missing from the source files before reporting it.

## 5\. Proposed MVP Assumptions

Propose the minimum assumptions needed to resolve important gaps and make the requirements actionable.

For each assumption, include:

- A unique ID (ASM-01, ASM-02, etc.).
- The proposed rule or behavior.
- Why it is a reasonable MVP default.
- Related gaps and source references.
- Risk if wrong: low, medium, or high.
- A simple way to validate it.

Consider authentication, identifiers, permissions, registration, roster locking, check-in, bracket generation, score confirmation, no-shows, external imports, disputes, and tournament completion where relevant.

Clearly distinguish assumptions from confirmed requirements.

## Working Guidelines

- Base the analysis on all files in `docs/`, not just the project overview.
- Preserve existing story IDs and scenario names. Never invent references.
- Quote original requirements accurately, preserving their original language.
- Be specific and evidence-driven. Avoid generic recommendations.
- Separate explicit requirements, reasonable inferences, and unresolved questions.
- Do not introduce new features or expand the agreed MVP scope.
- Do not select technologies or propose implementation architecture.
- Focus on decisions that materially affect business logic, security, data integrity, or the end-to-end user journey.

## Output Structure

Write `docs/analysis/requirements-digest.md` with these five main sections:

1. Entities and Their Lifecycles
2. Business Rules and Invariants
3. Non-Functional Requirements
4. Gaps and Open Questions
5. Proposed MVP Assumptions

Finish with a short **MVP Readiness Summary** covering the critical unresolved decisions, major contradictions, assumptions requiring confirmation, and whether the current requirements are sufficient to implement the end-to-end MVP.

Before finishing, verify traceability, risk ratings, consistency, and that the output file exists at the requested path.

Be rigorous and practical. The goal is to establish what the requirements actually guarantee, what remains unclear, and what must be decided before development.
